01The problem

An independent artist's presence is usually scattered: a Linktree, a few platform profiles, and no place that tells the story in the artist's own voice. For an artist whose identity spans multiple genres and eras, that fragmentation actively works against the catalogue.

The brief: one destination that makes a first-time listener understand the artist in a scroll — and gives existing fans somewhere to go deeper.

02What I built

Artist storytelling

A narrative structure — prologue, story, and chapters — instead of a generic "about" page.

Era timeline

Five years of releases organized as chapters, so the evolution across genres reads as intent, not noise.

Discography

Albums and singles presented with their artwork and context, linked to streaming.

Visuals section

Music videos and visual content integrated into the experience.

Streaming ecosystem

One organized layer of links — Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, SoundCloud, and socials.

Fan engagement

A newsletter path for announcements, keeping the artist's audience his own.

03Status

Live at trillamelodies.com, updated as new music and visuals release.

  • Brand & identity
  • Interactive presentation
  • Discography system
  • Media integration
  • Responsive design

04What I learned

Personal brands need editorial judgment more than features. The hard work was deciding what the story was — which eras mattered, what order, what to leave out. The code is in service of that; a distinctive site is mostly a distinctive point of view, rendered carefully.
