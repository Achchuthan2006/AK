01The problem

A church serves two audiences at once: the family who has attended for twenty years and the visitor deciding whether to walk in for the first time. Add two languages and two services, and a static one-page site stops working — people need to find this week's events, catch a livestream, request prayer, and know exactly what to expect on a Sunday.

I know this community from the inside: I've volunteered here since 2023, coordinating event logistics and services. The platform is built from that first-hand understanding of how the congregation actually uses it.

02What the platform does

Bilingual by design

Tamil and English throughout — service listings, navigation, and content that respect both languages equally.

Services & visitor journey

Clear service times, what-to-expect guidance, and a visit-planning path for newcomers.

Sermons & livestreams

A sermon library with YouTube-integrated livestreams for both the English and Tamil services.

Ministries & events

Kids, youth, prayer & care, and outreach ministries, plus an events calendar the community actually keeps current.

Prayer & care workflows

Structured prayer-request and care forms, so reaching out doesn't depend on catching someone after service.

Music & media

Lyrics videos and music content integrated with the church's YouTube presence.

03How it serves the community

  1. FindA visitor or member finds service times, events, and directions — in their language.
  2. JoinLivestreams and the sermon library keep members connected from anywhere.
  3. Reach outPrayer and care requests arrive through structured forms the ministry team can act on.
  4. Keep currentEvents, sermons, and announcements are updated as community life moves.

04Status & what's next

The platform is live at praisetabernacle.org and updated continuously alongside the life of the community — events, sermons, and seasonal content.

05What I learned

Community software is maintained, not launched. The build matters less than the rhythm after it: making weekly updates easy enough that they actually happen. Designing for the volunteer who updates the events page is as important as designing for the visitor who reads it.
