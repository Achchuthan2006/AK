Work/Wanted for Fries

A 3D chase story that started as a birthday gift.

Wanted for Fries is an independent, narrative-driven 3D game: a getaway story told through vehicle chases, cinematic sequences, and a world built one system at a time. It began as a birthday project and grew into a full exercise in game architecture — physics, animation, cameras, audio, and level progression.

01The idea

Most portfolio projects prove you can follow a tutorial. A game proves you can make a hundred systems cooperate: input, physics, cameras, AI, animation, audio, and story all running in the same frame budget. Wanted for Fries is deliberately ambitious for an independent build — a comedic chase narrative with real gameplay systems underneath it.

It's positioned honestly: an independent project by one builder, not a studio production — which is exactly what makes it a useful demonstration of engineering range.

02Systems built

Vehicle mechanics

Driveable vehicles with handling tuned for chase gameplay rather than simulation.

Police chase AI

Pursuit behavior that escalates with the player's actions — the core tension loop of the game.

Cinematic sequences

In-engine cutscenes and camera work that carry the story between gameplay beats.

Character & procedural animation

Character animation blended with procedural techniques for reactive movement.

Audio & VFX

Sound design and visual effects layered into gameplay and cinematics.

Level progression

A staged structure that introduces mechanics before combining them.

03Why it matters in a portfolio

Games are the harshest systems-engineering test available to a solo developer: everything is real-time, everything is stateful, and nothing can block the frame. The habits it builds — profiling, decoupling systems, designing for iteration — transfer directly to product engineering.

  • 3D engine
  • Gameplay systems
  • Physics
  • Cinematics
  • Procedural animation
  • Audio & VFX

04Status & what's next

In active development. Current focus: tightening the chase loop and completing the level progression. Gameplay footage will be added here as the build matures.

05What I learned

Scope is a design material. The game only stayed alive because each system was cut to the smallest version that still served the story — a discipline that applies to every product I build: ship the loop, then deepen it.
