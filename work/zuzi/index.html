Work/ZUZI

Modernizing a fashion storefront, product by product.

ZUZI is a Sri Lankan fashion retail brand with an established online store. This project is my independent modernization demonstration of its storefront: a rebuilt catalogue, product detail pages, variants, cart and wishlist, and a mobile-first shopping experience — engineering practice on a real brand's real catalogue shape.

01Context — and an honest line

ZUZI operates its own production store at zuzi.lk, built and run by the brand. This case study documents something different: my independent demonstration of how that storefront could be modernized — the catalogue architecture, product experience, and mobile shopping flow rebuilt with current e-commerce engineering practice.

It is presented as exactly that: a demonstration and engineering exercise on a real catalogue's shape, not the brand's official website and not a commissioned replacement.

Fashion commerce lives or dies on the product page — imagery, variants, and the two taps between wanting and buying.

02What the demonstration covers

Fashion catalogue

Collection and category architecture that scales with seasonal drops and keeps browsing fast.

Product detail pages

Merchandising-first product pages — imagery leads, details support, and variants stay legible.

Variants & selection

Sizes and colourways modelled as structured data, with availability reflected in the UI, not discovered at checkout.

Cart & wishlist

Persistent cart and wishlist flows designed for the browse-now-buy-later rhythm of fashion shopping.

Navigation & search

Findability across collections — search, filtering, and a navigation system that works at catalogue scale.

Accessibility & testing

Semantic structure, keyboard support, and automated tests over the flows that earn the revenue.

03How it's engineered

The storefront is structured the way modern retail platforms are: products and variants as schema-driven data, server-rendered pages for SEO and speed, and the mobile experience treated as the primary surface rather than a squeezed-down desktop.

  1. BrowseCollections and search surface the catalogue; product cards stay image-led and fast.
  2. DecideProduct pages present imagery, variants, and availability without burying the buy action.
  3. HoldCart and wishlist persist the shopper's intent across sessions and devices.
  4. Check outA checkout-ready order structure carries variant, pricing, and fulfilment data cleanly.
  • Product & variant schema
  • Server rendering
  • Cart & wishlist state
  • Search & filtering
  • Automated testing
  • Mobile-first UI

04Status & what's next

The demonstration build is in active development and not yet publicly deployed; screenshots and a walkthrough will be added here once it reaches a presentable milestone. ZUZI's own production store remains the brand's official storefront at zuzi.lk. Brand imagery will be used here only where available and authorized — until then, this page deliberately uses neutral type-led visuals.

05What this project exercises

Fashion retail is the most demanding flavour of e-commerce UX: deep catalogues, fast-moving inventory, and shoppers who decide in seconds on a phone. Rebuilding a real brand's storefront shape — rather than a toy shop — forces the catalogue modelling, variant logic, and merchandising decisions that production retail actually requires.
