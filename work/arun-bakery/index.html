01The problem

A beloved bakery can be invisible online. Menus live in phone calls and word of mouth, ordering means waiting on hold, and none of it works in the language many customers prefer. Generic ordering apps take a cut and flatten the brand into a listing.

The goal here is a platform the bakery owns: its brand, its menu, its orders, its customer relationships — bilingual from the ground up, not translated as an afterthought.

02What I built

Bilingual experience

Tamil and English as equal first-class languages across menus, pages, and ordering — reflecting how customers actually speak.

Menu system

Structured menu management so items, categories, prices, and availability are updated by the bakery, not by a developer.

Online ordering with Square

An ordering workflow with Square payments, designed for pickup patterns and busy weekend rushes.

Promotions & notifications

Seasonal promotions and customer notifications for the moments that matter to a bakery calendar.

AI voice receptionist (concept)

A bilingual voice-ordering concept: answer calls, take orders, and hand structured order data to the same system.

Franchise-ready structure

Content and data modelled so a second location doesn't mean a second codebase.

03How it works

  1. DiscoverCustomers browse the brand site and menu in Tamil or English, on the phone in their hand.
  2. OrderItems are configured and paid for through the Square-backed ordering workflow — or, in the voice concept, spoken over the phone.
  3. FulfilOrders arrive as structured data the bakery can act on; promotions and notifications close the loop with customers.
  4. ManageMenu, pricing, and promotions are managed through admin tooling built for non-technical staff.
  • Bilingual EN/TA content

04Status & what's next

The current development build is available above. The ordering workflow and menu system remain the focus, while the AI voice receptionist is at concept-and-prototype stage.

05What I learned

Bilingual isn't a toggle — it's an architecture decision. Modelling every piece of content in two languages from day one is far cheaper than retrofitting it, and it forces cleaner content structure everywhere else. It's also a real differentiator: very few local businesses get software that speaks their customers' language.
